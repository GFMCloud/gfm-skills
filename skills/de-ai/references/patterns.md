# Patterns that make prose read machine-written

Each entry: what it looks like, then the usual fix. Fix only what is there; most texts show a few of
these, not all.

## Contents

- A. Staging instead of saying (1 to 5)
- B. Rhythm by rule (6 to 9)
- C. Inflation and borrowed weight (10 to 15)
- D. Formatting by habit (16 to 19)
- E. Chat and draft leftovers (20 to 23)

## A. Staging instead of saying

1. **Contrast setups.** "It's not just a tool, it's a movement." "This isn't about speed. It's about
   trust." Fix: state the positive claim alone, if it is true.
2. **Run-ups.** "Here's the thing:", "Let's be clear:", "The truth is," "What's interesting is that".
   Fix: delete; start at the point.
3. **Dramatic closers.** A short final line that restates the paragraph as a slogan ("And that changes
   everything."). Fix: delete, or keep only if it adds a fact.
4. **Questions nobody asked.** "So what does this mean for you?" followed by the answer. Fix: give the
   answer as a statement.
5. **Sayings that sound deep.** "Data is the new oil." "Slow is smooth, smooth is fast." Fix: delete,
   or replace with the specific claim the saying stands in for.

## B. Rhythm by rule

6. **Threes by habit.** "fast, reliable, and scalable" when only one was shown. Fix: keep the items the
   text supports; two or four are fine.
7. **Same-shape sentences.** Every sentence opens with the subject and runs the same length, or the
   same opener repeats ("This...", "This...", "This..."). Fix: merge or vary only where it reads badly.
8. **Em dashes everywhere** (the long dash, U+2014, and the spaced en dash used the same way). Fix: a
   comma, full stop, colon or brackets, whichever the sentence needs.
9. **Stacked qualifiers and hyphen pairs.** "truly robust, deeply integrated, end-to-end". Fix: keep the
   one word that carries meaning.

## C. Inflation and borrowed weight

10. **Stock vocabulary.** delve, leverage (as a verb), seamless, robust, pivotal, crucial, landscape,
    realm, tapestry, testament, unlock, elevate, empower, game-changer, cutting-edge, navigate (for
    anything but travel). Fix: the plain word ("use", "important", "area"), or nothing.
11. **Inflated significance.** "marks a pivotal moment", "plays a vital role in shaping". Fix: say what
    happened and let the reader judge.
12. **Vague connection.** "is closely tied to", "has implications for" with no stated link. Fix: name
    the link or delete.
13. **Trailing -ing riders.** "..., highlighting the importance of X", "..., ensuring Y". Fix: delete,
    or make it its own sentence with a subject if it is a real claim.
14. **Sales voice.** "boasts", "nestled", "a must-have", "takes it to the next level". Fix: describe
    the feature.
15. **Borrowed authority.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "it is widely recognised" with no source.
    Fix: cite the source if the text has one; otherwise drop the appeal and keep the claim, hedged
    if the author hedged. Don't give the claim a new owner ("We think...") or tie it to the product
    with a sentence of your own.

## D. Formatting by habit

16. **Decorative bold.** Bold on ordinary phrases rather than on the one thing a reader must not miss.
    Fix: unbold.
17. **Headings for scraps.** A heading over one or two sentences, or a heading the first sentence
    repeats. Fix: merge into the surrounding text.
18. **Bullets for prose.** A list where each bullet is a clause of one sentence. Fix: write the
    sentence. Keep lists for real steps or parallel items.
19. **Emoji signposts and curly quotes** in plain text that otherwise uses straight quotes. Fix:
    remove the emoji; match the document's quote style.

## E. Chat and draft leftovers

20. **Chat openers and closers.** "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if
    you'd like...". Fix: delete.
21. **Knowledge-limit notes.** "As of my last update", "I don't have real-time data". Fix: delete, or
    replace with the actual date of the fact if the author gave one.
22. **Talking about the draft.** "This revised version...", "Below is an improved...". Fix: delete.
23. **Placeholder residue.** "[Insert example]", "Company X", unfinished template text. Fix: leave it
    and list it under `Kept:` so the author fills it in; never invent the missing content.
